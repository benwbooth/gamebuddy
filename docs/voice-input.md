# Microphone activation

GameBuddy owns microphone activation and overlay focus. Lunchpail supplies the game launch context and emulator configuration.

**Hands-free** is enabled by default when launching a game with GameBuddy, including through Lunchpail. An already-unmuted microphone starts listening automatically: just speak, without clicking the overlay or toggling mute first. A muted microphone stays closed until you unmute it. **Stop listening** closes capture and stays stopped; a later system mute/unmute gesture or **Listen** can resume it. Disable **Hands-free** to use only Listen or hold-to-talk. Enabling it again starts listening if the selected microphone is unmuted. Opening the standalone companion/workbench does not automatically listen; `--no-listen` also starts a launched game with Hands-free disabled.

With Codex live voice configured, a launched game automatically connects for a short introduction and occasional next-action cues. With the microphone muted or listening disabled, output-only mode sends a generated silent track to keep the realtime transport clock advancing without opening a microphone. Muting stops capture but leaves guide speech available. **Voice off** disables output, **Stop** pauses the voice session, and `--quiet` starts with output disabled (listening is controlled separately). The local Whisper/Qt backend also starts listening automatically and retains its existing voice quality.

Automatic hints are at least 90 seconds apart, require a stable action for 12 seconds, and mention each action at most once per game session. Routine menus, dialogue and area changes stay silent. Only repeated confident death, danger or victory observations can produce an event cue, with a five-minute cooldown per event type. Automatic speech pauses during conversation. Confirmed critical events may speak three seconds afterward and at least eight seconds after the previous automatic cue; routine next-action hints wait 45 seconds. The task visible during conversation is not repeated as a delayed hint. These limits do not delay requested replies. Both reasoning paths are instructed to answer in one or two short sentences unless asked for detail, placing longer instructions in widgets. Refreshing the OSD never generates a spoken answer by itself.

Live voice playback requires a completed backend answer or a selected application cue. Player speech starts a request but does not authorize the voice model to improvise gameplay directions. The application dispatches recognized, finished requests directly; it never waits for a speech-model acknowledgment or delegation. It buffers the backend's final answer, rejects superseded utterances, and refreshes native screen/map evidence when the view changes during reasoning. A result that arrives during continued player speech waits for the pause or corrected request instead of disappearing. Completed text appears immediately in the companion, independently of speech playback. Before approved speech starts, any older unapproved voice turn and its incoming audio tail must finish; those samples are discarded. Only the approved turn may play. Native turn completion and buffered-audio completion return it to waiting, without a 12- or 20-second speech cutoff. Long-form requests therefore wait for their completed backend text before playback. An app cue that produces neither a turn nor audio is retried once after six seconds. No playable audio after twelve seconds produces a visible text fallback, even if an empty turn was created; that retry never replays tools or player requests. An older turn blocking the speech queue is allowed to drain while audio arrives, but six seconds without audio or ninety seconds of total waiting releases the queue with text fallback. Failed starts, failed turns and a sixty-second reasoning deadline release the waiting state with a visible error while the microphone stays available. The deadline does not limit already-playing speech. Background perception continues throughout. Saved dialogue is historical context, not a conversation to continue.

An unfinished reasoning turn is cancelled when a new player utterance begins, so a slow answer cannot finish over a newer question. Pauses between clauses of the same request do not cancel its work.

The live microphone remains active through replies, using Chromium acoustic echo cancellation, noise suppression and automatic gain control. You can interrupt an answer or reply immediately after it. During audible output, local speech activity must also have recognized incoming speech before it cuts off playback, which filters brief residual echo. Short interruptions remain eligible when recognition arrives just after local speech ends. A completed “stop”, “stop talking”, “stop speaking” or “be quiet” silences output and returns to listening; a longer request beginning with “stop” still receives its answer. Mute and Stop listening still close capture. The companion distinguishes listening, checking a request, waiting for voice and speaking.

Playback permission changes apply immediately. Where Chromium supports `RTCRtpReceiver.jitterBufferTarget`, the receiver requests 300 ms of native audio buffering to retain the response onset while the local speech detector releases its final 200 ms. This is a browser playout target, not a guaranteed network latency. A bounded 30-second audio queue also retains an answer that starts while the player is still speaking. It releases queued samples when the player pauses, drops deliberately muted audio, and clears interrupted audio for a replacement reply. The remote track keeps a muted native decoder, while Web Audio plays the queue. The microphone retains Chromium echo cancellation.

Live assistant captions use the voice data channel's `start_ms` transcript timestamps and the Web Audio queue's sample positions. Text waits until the corresponding samples reach scheduled playback, including output-device latency; a completed transcript cannot flush unplayed words onto the screen. The first voiced samples anchor each reply's timeline. Interruptions discard queued text and audio together. Captions show the latest 240 characters and linger for eight seconds after playback ends. With output muted, approved text streams without waiting for audio that is deliberately discarded. The companion and conversation archive retain the approved answer.

The collapsed icon turns red and animates while capturing, including WebRTC capture. An amber dot indicates an active conversation paused for processing or playback. Starting a conversation restores a hidden icon but does not expand the overlay or change game input focus. Muting closes capture and submits any sufficiently long speech already buffered; silence and short transients are discarded. Existing widgets remain visible. Device changes or loss of mute-state visibility stop automatic capture.

For the local Whisper backend, speech is segmented using [WebRTC VAD through libfvad](https://github.com/kaegi/webrtc-vad), with a 200 ms pre-roll, at least 300 ms of detected speech, a 900 ms pause to end a turn, and a 25-second maximum turn. Audio comes from [Qt QAudioSource](https://doc.qt.io/qt-6/qaudiosource.html) through a small CXX bridge; activation, segmentation and WAV storage are Rust. The current capture format is 16 kHz mono PCM. A device that cannot provide this format reports an error and retains the hold-to-talk fallback.

Whisper transcribes each turn locally, then the configured reasoning provider receives the text and game context. Capture pauses while that voice turn is processed and while Qt speaks the reply, with an 800 ms recovery interval. This is turn-based conversation, not full-duplex interruption or acoustic echo cancellation. A headset helps keep game dialogue out of the microphone; VAD cannot distinguish the player from other audible speech.

Capture can continue during unrelated guide/perception jobs. One completed speech turn waits for the current job, then gets priority over the next automatic frame match. Audio is removed after transcription, including recognition/provider failures. A queued recording is discarded on orderly application shutdown. An abrupt process kill can leave a temporary WAV in the app's `voice` directory.

Explicit spoken commands bypass the reasoning provider:

| Command | Result |
| --- | --- |
| “Open the guide” | Opens the walkthrough and takes overlay focus |
| “Arrange widgets” | Opens interactive widget arrangement |
| “Back to the game” / “Close the guide” | Releases overlay interaction |
| “Hide the overlay” | Hides surfaces and releases focus |
| “Show the overlay” | Restores surfaces |

Ordinary strategy questions do not take focus. Shift+Tab, Ctrl+Shift+Tab and Escape remain available.

## Automatic game volume

**Lower game volume during conversations** is enabled by default in Options → Models (`duck_game_audio` in settings). A launched game's audio fades down by **12 dB** when microphone speech is detected, while a local voice request is processed, while hold-to-talk records, or when GameBuddy speaks. An unmuted but silent microphone and an idle voice connection do not lower it. The game returns to its normal level after 1.8 seconds without conversation activity, with a short release fade. Realtime voice holds through an active reasoning handoff and the pending spoken answer, then releases after the completed conversation.

For realtime input, the existing WebRTC/libfvad detector receives ephemeral 20 ms, 16 kHz samples from the already-authorized Chromium microphone stream. No second microphone is opened; Qt consumes the activity messages locally without writing audio files or application traces. Output activity comes from the actual received audio, respecting Voice off, rather than the presence of an audio track. Local voice uses its existing VAD and Qt speech state. Closing a voice session clears activity.

The current volume backend requires **Linux with PipeWire**, including applications using its PulseAudio compatibility server. It follows the launch PID and descendants, including uniquely identifiable container processes. It applies temporary per-stream software attenuation; it never changes system volume or the application's saved mixer level. New game streams are discovered during conversations. A manual mixer volume adjustment takes precedence for that stream until the conversation ends. Ambiguous process ownership is left untouched.

A separate Rust helper owns the attenuation and restores surviving streams when the overlay closes or crashes. A three-second heartbeat timeout also releases it if the UI stops responding. Disabling the option releases the helper immediately. The helper uses `pw-dump` and `pw-cli`, already present in the Nix runtime. Windows/macOS and a standalone PulseAudio server do not yet have ducking adapters; the settings page reports their availability. Unrelated apps and GameBuddy output are outside the launched game's process tree.

`nix develop --command cargo run -p gamebuddy-services --example audio_duck_probe` checks actual output attenuation on a private virtual sink, restoration, channel balance, manual volume changes, recreated streams and helper EOF. `scripts/test-realtime` also checks ducking through the production WebRTC microphone and spoken introduction, including silence with an active microphone. Both fixtures avoid physical audio devices.

## Platform boundaries

Automatic system mute detection currently supports Linux PulseAudio and PipeWire's PulseAudio compatibility server. A Rust background worker queries the exact Qt-selected source through `pactl` every 250 ms, with a bounded timeout; it never changes the system mute state. The Nix shell includes `pactl`. A physical microphone's mute button must expose its state through that server to trigger activation. The Shure MV7+ was identified as the host's default input, but its physical mute button has not been tested.

The shared Qt capture path and Listen/hold-to-talk controls remain available to other platforms, but Windows/macOS system mute adapters and native runtime verification are pending. Qt microphone permissions and macOS packaging declarations still apply. `GAMEBUDDY_AUDIO_SOURCE` pins an explicit Qt device ID; disappearance never silently switches capture to another device. Without it, GameBuddy follows the default input and requires renewed activation after a device change.

## Verification

Run `nix develop --command scripts/test-voice` from the project. The test creates a temporary virtual sink/source, keeps the user's default input unchanged, feeds synthesized speech through Qt capture and the real local Whisper model, and launches the production overlay in a private Gamescope session. `scripts/test-gamescope` separately covers keyboard, icon and HTML widget interactions. Set `GAMEBUDDY_WHISPER_MODEL` to an existing model to avoid a fixture-local download.

The local test starts muted and checks mute/unmute capture lifecycle, passive focus, a spoken guide command, a question through a delayed local provider fixture, capture suspension during processing, resumption, and recording cleanup. `scripts/test-realtime` starts its virtual mic unmuted before launch, then sends a spoken request without clicking or toggling mute. Its synthetic player runs outside the game process tree so game ducking cannot attenuate the test microphone. `GAMEBUDDY_TEST_RESPONSIVE=1 scripts/test-realtime` adds a spoken interruption with a new screenshot/widget request and a long answer whose final words must appear in the recorded output. It checks real subscription voice, screen tools, widgets, audio ducking, and capture closure on mute. Unit tests check that Stop, device changes, monitoring failures and capture failures do not trigger automatic restarts. Non-voice fixtures use `--no-listen`. The provider fixture checks integration, not model answer quality. `--voice-trace` logs capture, native event types, tool timings, packet counters and speech-state transitions without microphone samples or SDP. Debug traces include the approved assistant captions and speech text. Physical microphone acoustics and hardware mute-button behavior need a separate hands-on check.

Set `GAMEBUDDY_TEST_CAPTIONS=1` with `scripts/test-realtime` to require captions emitted by the playback queue, capture their rendered appearance, and verify the opening words “Purple elephants” in recorded output. It can be combined with `GAMEBUDDY_TEST_RESPONSIVE=1`.

A recorded-scene voice regression accepts `GAMEBUDDY_TEST_SCENE=/path/to/cropped-game.jpg` and `GAMEBUDDY_TEST_STORE=/tmp/disposable-prepared-store` with `scripts/test-realtime`. It renders the image as the private game window, calibrates its letterbox, waits for real atlas registration, plants old movement advice, then asks the gate question through the virtual microphone. It requires both screen/map images, a key interaction in the shared advice, played captions, and the word “key” in recorded audio. The current scenario is Faxanadu-specific test data, not production game logic.
